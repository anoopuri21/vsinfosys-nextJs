What should a business prepare before starting a website?

A practical website project checklist covering goals, audience, content, domain, hosting, functionality and measurement.

A practical website project checklist covering goals, audience, content, domain, hosting, functionality and measurement.

Define the business action

Decide what the website should help a visitor do: understand a service, request a proposal, buy a product, book a conversation or use a tool. One primary action creates a stronger foundation for structure and content.

Describe the audience

List the people the website must serve, what they already know, the questions they ask and what could prevent them from acting. Avoid defining an audience as everyone.

Collect business facts

Prepare the official name, contact details, service area, business hours, service list, process, policies and any claims that can be verified. Missing facts should remain missing rather than being invented.

Inventory content and assets

Collect existing copy, documents, logos, photographs, videos, product information and old URLs. Mark what is current, what needs revision and what does not exist yet.

List functional requirements

Forms, commerce, logins, dashboards, calculators, languages and integrations affect architecture. Separate essential launch requirements from later improvements.

Secure access and ownership

The business should control the domain, hosting, analytics and platform accounts. Record renewals and recovery methods.

Agree on measurement

Choose useful outcomes such as qualified enquiries, purchases, booked calls or completed tasks. Total traffic alone does not define success.

A useful platform or automation decision begins with the work it must support.

This guide provides general planning information. A final recommendation requires the real business, content, hosting and workflow constraints.
