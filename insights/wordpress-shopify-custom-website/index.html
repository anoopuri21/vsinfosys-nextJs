Custom website, WordPress or Shopify: how to choose

A practical comparison of custom static websites, WordPress and Shopify for business owners planning a new digital presence.

A practical comparison of custom static websites, WordPress and Shopify for business owners planning a new digital presence.

Start with the operating model—not the platform

The right platform depends on what the website must do, who will update it, how often it changes, where it will be hosted and what the business can maintain. Choosing only from popularity can create unnecessary cost later.

Choose a custom static website when

A custom HTML, CSS and JavaScript website can be an excellent fit when speed, portability, distinctive design and low hosting cost matter more than frequent non-technical editing. It has a small attack surface and can run on inexpensive static or shared hosting. Content updates need a controlled editing or deployment process.

Choose WordPress when

WordPress is useful when a team needs familiar browser-based editing, structured publishing and a large ecosystem. It works well on standard PHP/MySQL hosting, but theme quality, plugin discipline, caching and maintenance directly affect performance and security.

Choose Shopify when

Shopify is designed for managed commerce. It is often suitable when catalogue, checkout, payments and store operations are the centre of the project. Monthly platform and app costs should be evaluated alongside the reduced infrastructure responsibility.

Questions to decide

Who will update content? Is checkout required? Are custom workflows essential? What is the hosting budget? How important is platform portability? Which integrations are mandatory? The answer should emerge from these constraints.

A practical conclusion

There is no universally best platform. A smaller, well-matched system usually outperforms a larger system the team cannot operate confidently.

A useful platform or automation decision begins with the work it must support.

This guide provides general planning information. A final recommendation requires the real business, content, hosting and workflow constraints.
