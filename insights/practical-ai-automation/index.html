Where can AI save time—and where should it not be used?

A practical framework for finding responsible AI automation opportunities in small and medium business workflows.

A practical framework for finding responsible AI automation opportunities in small and medium business workflows.

Begin with a repeated process

The strongest starting point is usually a process that happens frequently, follows understandable steps and consumes time through classification, retrieval, drafting or routing.

Map inputs, decisions and exceptions

Document what enters the process, which decisions are made, where information comes from and what unusual cases look like. If the process cannot be explained, automating it may make the confusion faster.

Keep human review where consequences matter

Financial, legal, medical, safety, privacy and reputation-sensitive outputs need appropriate control. Human review is also valuable when context and relationship matter more than speed.

Evaluate data before the model

Check whether the required information is accurate, accessible and permitted for the proposed tool. Security, retention, provider terms and confidentiality should be part of the design.

Prototype a narrow outcome

Test one clear task before connecting an entire operation. Compare time, quality, error patterns and user confidence with the current process.

Know when not to automate

Do not automate a rare process with unclear value, a sensitive decision without oversight, or a broken workflow that first needs simplification.

A useful test

AI is appropriate when it makes a defined task meaningfully easier while keeping quality, privacy and accountability within acceptable boundaries.

A useful platform or automation decision begins with the work it must support.

This guide provides general planning information. A final recommendation requires the real business, content, hosting and workflow constraints.
