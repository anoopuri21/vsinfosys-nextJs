## 1. Purpose of This Plan

This document defines the complete homepage architecture, content direction, visual system, motion behavior, conversion path and responsive strategy for VS Infosys.

The homepage must function as:

1. a clear introduction to VS Infosys;
2. a premium demonstration of its design and development capability;
3. a structured guide through a broad service offering;
4. a useful resource for visitors who are not sure what they need; and
5. a focused path toward a genuine project enquiry.

The final experience should feel like a **cinematic digital product demonstration**, not a conventional agency template.

### Non-negotiable content rules

The homepage will not:

- invent projects, clients, reviews, awards or results;
- use fictional work that could be mistaken for client work;
- mention that the company is new;
- make unsupported market-leadership claims;
- use fabricated traffic, revenue or conversion graphs; or
- create empty Portfolio, Work, Clients or Testimonials sections.

Confidence will come from **craft, clarity, useful demonstrations, process transparency and technical standards**.

## 3. Review of the Previous Plan

The previous direction was strategically useful, but it needed correction before UI design.

### 3.1 What was already strong

- The website itself was positioned as the primary proof of capability.
- The black-and-white visual system with controlled glowing light was distinctive.
- Services were grouped instead of displayed as 13 identical cards.
- Pinned storytelling, parallax and connected motion had a clear narrative role.
- Fake portfolio and social-proof content had been removed.
- The plan considered performance, accessibility and reduced motion.

### 3.2 Problems found

#### Too many sections were competing for equal attention

A cinematic homepage needs rhythm. If every section is large, animated and visually intense, nothing feels important. The new plan assigns each section an intensity level and introduces quiet editorial chapters between major experiences.

#### Some sections overlapped

The Outcome Navigator, Engagement Paths and Who We Work Best With repeated similar decision-making content. They now have distinct jobs:

- Capability Navigator answers **“What outcome do I need?”**
- Engagement Fit answers **“Is VS Infosys appropriate for my situation?”**
- Project Starter answers **“How do I begin?”**

#### The final section contained too many unrelated functions

FAQ, Delhi positioning, enquiry and footer were previously grouped as one section. They now have separate pacing and responsibilities.

#### Motion scope was too ambitious

Several pinned chapters, WebGL scenes and continuous effects could create fatigue and performance risk. The revised plan permits only three signature scroll sequences. All other motion is lightweight and supportive.

#### Technology appeared too prominently

Clients generally buy outcomes rather than frameworks. Technology remains important, but it is moved below service value, standards and process.

#### Some services could become visually hidden

Domain and hosting, WordPress, dynamic websites, app development, graphics and video generation needed more explicit placement. The revised architecture maps every service to a visible homepage chapter and deeper page route.

#### “Insights” could look artificial without an existing publication history

The launch version will show only three genuinely useful guides. It will not imitate a mature magazine archive.

#### The Interactive Capability Lab needed clearer framing

It is not a portfolio or collection of fictional projects. It is now designed as a live educational demonstration with neutral interface systems and explanatory labels.

## 6. Homepage Narrative

The homepage follows five acts.

### Act I — The Signal

The visitor understands the company and sees the visual system begin.

### Act II — The Possibilities

The visitor explores what VS Infosys can create and how the services connect.

### Act III — The Standard

The website demonstrates design quality, technical discipline and tangible deliverables.

### Act IV — The Partnership

The visitor understands the process, fit, technology choices and likely engagement.

### Act V — The Decision

The visitor receives useful answers and a clear route into a project conversation.

### Recurring visual metaphor

A white-hot point emits an orange-white light trace.

The trace becomes:

- an orbit in the hero;
- a navigation path;
- a website grid;
- a commerce journey;
- a search/content cluster;
- a media waveform;
- an AI workflow;
- a product data connection;
- a process timeline; and
- a direct path into the enquiry form.

This gives the entire homepage one cinematic identity.

# 9. Decorative and Atmospheric System

Decorative elements must support hierarchy and narrative.

## 9.1 Light Trace

The primary recurring element. It connects scenes, indicates active states and resolves into the final CTA.

## 9.2 Fine Grid

Used behind technical or spatial visuals. It should never cover every section. Grid density changes according to chapter:

- vertical in the hero;
- modular in web design;
- coordinate-based near Delhi/contact;
- node-based in SEO and AI.

## 9.3 Film Grain

A low-opacity static texture adds cinematic material without visible noise movement. It must not reduce text clarity.

## 9.4 Glass Objects

Translucent monochrome forms represent systems, products and layers. Each object uses the same lighting direction.

## 9.5 Scene Numbers

Small section numbers such as `03 / 19` reinforce the editorial structure and help orient visitors during cinematic chapters.

## 9.6 Progress Rail

Used only in the Digital Lifecycle, service chapter sequence and VS Method. It should not remain fixed across the entire homepage.

## 9.7 Light Nodes

Small white-hot points indicate decisions, connections and active workflow steps. They should not become random particle decoration.

## 9.8 Masked Transitions

A light shape may expand to reveal a black-to-white chapter transition. Limit this effect to two uses on the homepage.

## 9.9 Image Depth Window

The outer frame remains stable while inner visual layers move subtly. This provides parallax depth without moving surrounding text.

## 9.10 Cursor Response

Desktop-only optional glow response inside demonstration areas. Do not replace the standard cursor or apply a trailing cursor across the entire website.

# 11. Motion Architecture

## Signature sequences

Only three sections receive large cinematic treatment:

1. Hero
2. Digital Lifecycle
3. Practical AI

This prevents animation fatigue and protects performance.

## Medium interactions

- Capability Navigator
- Web interface assembly
- Commerce journey
- Growth node cluster
- Content branching
- Capability Lab
- Deliverable stack
- VS Method

## Quiet sections

- Point of View
- Build Standards
- Engagement Fit
- Technology
- Practical Guides
- FAQ

## Motion implementation priority

1. Custom CSS for hover, focus, reveals and visual effects
2. Native sticky positioning for pinned layouts
3. Vanilla JavaScript with IntersectionObserver for entry states
4. Small requestAnimationFrame modules for approved scroll-linked sequences
5. SVG or Canvas only when regular HTML/CSS becomes inefficient
6. WebGL only if the hero object cannot meet the intended quality efficiently through CSS/SVG and still passes the shared-hosting performance budget

The homepage prototype uses no animation framework or third-party runtime.

## Prohibited motion patterns

- Scroll-jacking
- Forced snap across the entire page
- Continuous text movement behind body content
- Every heading animating character by character
- Large autoplay video on mobile
- Uncontrolled particle fields
- Parallax on essential text
- Interactions available only through hover

## Reduced-motion version

- No parallax
- No long pinned transformations
- Static key visuals
- Short opacity transitions
- Immediate interface state changes
- No autoplay visual loops
- All content visible in normal document order

# 15. Performance and Accessibility Standards

## Performance targets

- LCP at or below 2.5 seconds
- INP at or below 200 milliseconds
- CLS at or below 0.1
- Production Lighthouse performance target of 90+ on representative pages
- Hero text rendered before enhancement assets
- Explicit media dimensions
- AVIF/WebP assets
- Lazy-loaded below-fold scenes
- Maximum one active canvas at a time

## Accessibility standards

- WCAG 2.2 AA direction
- Semantic reading order
- Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls
- Visible focus states
- Minimum 44px touch targets
- Verified contrast
- Reduced-motion support
- No flashing glow
- Decorative visuals ignored by assistive technology
- Form labels and errors announced correctly
- Pinned sections do not trap focus

# 16. Content and Hosting Architecture

The homepage prototype is fully static and does not require a CMS or database.

## Approved prototype

- Semantic HTML5
- Custom CSS
- Vanilla JavaScript modules
- Static/shared hosting

## Production options

1. **Static HTML/CSS/JavaScript + PHP form** for the lowest operating cost
2. **Custom WordPress theme** when frequent non-technical content editing is required
3. **Custom PHP/MySQL** when shared-hosting application logic is required without WordPress

If an editing system is selected, it should control homepage copy, capability routes, services, standards, process steps, guides, FAQs, contact details, navigation, footer and SEO metadata. Motion timing and technical animation behavior remain governed by the frontend design system rather than unrestricted editor controls.

The VS Infosys homepage should not attempt to appear established through invented history or borrowed proof. - commercially clear;
